# The pitch, and where it is said

One source for the words. The site, the Qwencoin pages, the README, the package and the link previews
say these, shortened to fit where needed, never changed in what they claim. Nothing here promises a
price or a profit, and nothing may be added that does.

The pitch in the author's words (pinned post, 2026-11-14): BTC-quoted memes "in order to further my
aim of arming humanity with pockets of people who have both a btc or btcish node (for record keeping
of value and messages without value) and local models (w the collective knowledge of mankind, to
rebuild) such that when the lights gps and internet go out, life will find a way."

## The words

| name | text |
|---|---|
| `tagline` | The memes pay for the nodes. |
| `what` | Memes quoted in BTC. Mined, not minted. |
| `oneliner` | Memecoins that are each their own Bitcoin fork: mined, priced in BTC and in QWEN, paying their holders. Every coin puts a node and a local model on more machines. |
| `fact_chain` | Every coin here is its own Bitcoin fork. Nobody mints it: machines mine it. |
| `fact_prices` | Two prices per coin, one in BTC and one in QWEN. Two curves; take the cheaper one. |
| `fact_holders` | 80% of what a block owes is paid to a holder of that coin, in BTC or in QWEN. Hold and you earn. |
| `why` | Why: mining these puts a Bitcoin-style node and a local model on one more machine, and then another. Pockets of people who have both keep the record and the knowledge when the lights, GPS and internet go out. Life will find a way. |
| `pockets` | {n} machines run a node and a model right now. Add yours. |
| `coin` | {TICKER} is its own chain with two curves. Whoever mines a block pays what it owes: 80% to a holder of {TICKER}, 10% to its creator, 10% to hashfun. No floor: the curves are the only seller. |
| `mine` | This block is mined for you. You get its coins and you pay what it owes, 80% of it to a holder. |
| `launch` | A coin is a chain. Name it, and the network's machines run it and mine it. Launching costs nothing. |
| `honest` | No presale, no team allocation, no floor. A coin nobody mines stops. |

## Where

| place | what is there |
|---|---|
| Every page of the site, beside the name | `tagline` |
| Board (the first thing anyone sees), above the coins | `what` as the heading; `fact_chain`, `fact_prices`, `fact_holders`; `why` set apart; `pockets` when the site knows the number; the two ways in (mine a coin, make a coin). Full on a first visit, one line (`what` and `tagline`, with a way to open it) after that, never gone |
| A coin's page, under its name | `coin`, then the two price points |
| The mine panel | `mine` |
| The launch form | `launch`, and `honest` |
| An empty board | the full block, and the launch form's way in |
| Link previews (`<title>`, description, Open Graph and Twitter tags) | site: `what` and `oneliner`, with the site's share picture; a coin: its name and ticker, both price points and how far each curve is, with the coin's own picture |
| The Qwencoin pages (aichat.fyi), header | a link to /fun that says what is there: "fun: the coins these nodes mine" |
| What a Qwencoin model is told when it is offered the fun functions | one sentence from `fact_chain` and `fact_holders` |
| README of both projects, package description, repository description | `oneliner` |

`pockets`: the number is the count of staked identities on the Qwencoin graph (each is a node with a
model), read from the Qwencoin pages' `/graph/status.json` (`identities`). "Add yours" goes to the
Qwencoin project's install line. Shown only when the number could be read in the last few minutes;
a small number is shown as it is.
